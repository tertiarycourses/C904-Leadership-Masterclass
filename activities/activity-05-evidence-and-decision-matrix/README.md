# Activity 05: Evidence and Decision Matrix

Leadership Masterclass · C904 · v4.0 · 35 minutes

## Goal

In this activity, you will compare alternatives and test assumptions using a fictional Meridian Services situation. You will discuss different perspectives, practise a leadership response and produce a reusable work artifact. The exercise connects the topic concepts to an everyday team decision, and the debrief focuses on how your response affects other people.

## What you will build

A completed evidence and decision matrix worksheet, a peer observation checklist and a practical next action.

## Preparation

Read scenario.md. Open worksheet.docx or print worksheet.pdf. Work in pairs or triads; appoint an observer for roleplay. All organisations, people and numbers in these practice packs are fictional. No paid diagnostic tool is required.

## Steps

1. Record the decision, options, criteria and scoring direction before calculating. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

2. Calculate A = 0.4×4 + 0.3×3 + 0.3×4 and B = 0.4×3 + 0.3×5 + 0.3×2. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

3. Identify missing evidence and one bias that could affect the ratings. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

4. Change weights to 0.2/0.6/0.2 and recalculate: A = 3.4 and B = 4.0. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

5. Recommend a next action with uncertainty, owner and a condition that would trigger review. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

## Test it

Check each item in checklist.md, then ask a peer to identify one clear strength and one concrete improvement. Confirm that your proposed action has an owner and a review point.

## Debrief

Which evidence changed your view? What trade-off remains? Which behaviour will you practise at work? Compare sample-completed-worksheet.pdf after your first attempt; it illustrates one possible response.
