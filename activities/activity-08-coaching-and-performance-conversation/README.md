# Activity 08: Coaching and Performance Conversation

Leadership Masterclass · C904 · v4.0 · 35 minutes

## Goal

In this activity, you will create ownership through questions using a fictional Meridian Services situation. You will discuss different perspectives, practise a leadership response and produce a reusable work artifact. The exercise connects the topic concepts to an everyday team decision, and the debrief focuses on how your response affects other people.

## What you will build

A completed coaching and performance conversation worksheet, a peer observation checklist and a practical next action.

## Preparation

Read scenario.md. Open worksheet.docx or print worksheet.pdf. Work in pairs or triads; appoint an observer for roleplay. All organisations, people and numbers in these practice packs are fictional. No paid diagnostic tool is required.

## Steps

1. Ask the learner playing the analyst to define the desired outcome. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

2. Explore facts, workload and resources without assuming a lack of effort. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

3. Ask for at least two options before suggesting advice. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

4. Agree one small trial, the leader’s support and an observable review measure. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

5. Swap roles and reflect on when coaching, mentoring or a direct instruction would be appropriate. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

## Test it

Check each item in checklist.md, then ask a peer to identify one clear strength and one concrete improvement. Confirm that your proposed action has an owner and a review point.

## Debrief

Which evidence changed your view? What trade-off remains? Which behaviour will you practise at work? Compare sample-completed-worksheet.pdf after your first attempt; it illustrates one possible response.
