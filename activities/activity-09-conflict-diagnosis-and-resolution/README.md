# Activity 09: Conflict Diagnosis and Resolution

Leadership Masterclass · C904 · v4.0 · 35 minutes

## Goal

In this activity, you will choose a proportionate response using a fictional Meridian Services situation. You will discuss different perspectives, practise a leadership response and produce a reusable work artifact. The exercise connects the topic concepts to an everyday team decision, and the debrief focuses on how your response affects other people.

## What you will build

A completed conflict diagnosis and resolution worksheet, a peer observation checklist and a practical next action.

## Preparation

Read scenario.md. Open worksheet.docx or print worksheet.pdf. Work in pairs or triads; appoint an observer for roleplay. All organisations, people and numbers in these practice packs are fictional. No paid diagnostic tool is required.

## Steps

1. Separate task, process, resource and relationship elements in the disagreement. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

2. Write a neutral problem statement and invite each perspective. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

3. Choose a response based on stakes, time and willingness to engage. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

4. Rehearse a constructive opening and a pause-and-restart phrase if emotions rise. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

5. Agree an action, owner and review, or record the appropriate escalation route. Write your reasoning in the worksheet; compare it with your partner before moving on.

## Test it

Check each item in checklist.md, then ask a peer to identify one clear strength and one concrete improvement. Confirm that your proposed action has an owner and a review point.

## Debrief

Which evidence changed your view? What trade-off remains? Which behaviour will you practise at work? Compare sample-completed-worksheet.pdf after your first attempt; it illustrates one possible response.
